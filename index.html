Training &
health log

An ongoing log of training and health data - strength, tonnage, body weight, and whatever else gets tracked - updated automatically as new sessions come in. Two logging conventions changed early on: dumbbells started being counted as a pair instead of one hand, and the gym changed in July, swapping every stack for one with different increments. Everything below is corrected for both, and marks where the correction is a guess.

How these numbers were made

  1. Estimated 1RM uses Epley: load × (1 + reps/30), working sets only (warm-ups and drop sets excluded). Past 15 reps the formula starts extrapolating an unrealistic max, so the reps fed into it are capped at 15 — the set still appears on every chart, just as a conservative estimate rather than a literal one. Capped points are ringed with a dashed amber outline; hover for the actual rep count.
  2. Bodyweight lifts are charged at a fraction of an assumed body mass: pull-ups, chin-ups and dips at full, push-ups at 0.65. Change that assumption and every bodyweight number moves with it.
  3. Dumbbell loads before the dates in section 02 were doubled to match how they were logged afterwards. Five exercises, three of them certain.
  4. The index normalises each exercise to the median of its own first three sessions, so a machine that reads high does not outrank a barbell. Group index = mean across exercises trained that day, smoothed on a 45-day window.
  5. At the gym change the two eras are spliced by assuming no strength changed overnight on 22 July. Splice factors ranged from ×0.92 to ×1.44; the larger the factor, the less the two eras really have in common.
  6. Cardio, planks and dead hangs are excluded from tonnage and strength; they carry no comparable load.
  7. Sessions logged at a gym other than the usual two count fully towards every total, but are marked (hatched bars, hollow points, an "away" tag) since the equipment isn't the same stack being tracked elsewhere.
  8. Any set past 15 reps still contributes a (capped, conservative) point to the strength trend — see above — and its actual weight and reps also show in the Records table's "High-rep set" column, alongside full credit in tonnage and total reps.
